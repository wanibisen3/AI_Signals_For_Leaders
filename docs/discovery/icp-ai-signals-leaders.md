# Ideal Customer Profile: AI Signals for Leaders

## Primary User Persona
**The Overwhelmed Strategist / Product Leader**
A senior operator (e.g., VP of Product, Director of Strategy, Principal Engineer, or Head of Innovation) who is responsible for "seeing around corners" but is drowning in a cacophony of generic AI news, hype cycles, and superficial LinkedIn influencer posts. They don't need *more* news; they need to know *what it means for their specific domain*.

## Buyer Persona
**The Department Head / Functional VP**
Often the same as the user persona for individual licenses (expensed via corporate card), or an executive sponsor seeking to arm their leadership team with competitive intelligence without the enterprise cost of a consulting firm or bulk subscriptions like Gartner/Forrester.

## Company Type
**Mid-Market to Scale-up Tech Companies & Traditional Enterprises Undergoing Digital Transformation**
These companies are large enough to feel existential threat from competitors adopting AI faster than them, but nimble enough that individual leaders are still tasked with defining the strategic response. (Independent professionals are secondary; they lack the budget friction).

## Company Size
200 - 5,000 employees. (Too small = founders do everything, lack budget for niche tools. Too large = locked into massive enterprise MS/Google ecosystems or relying on dedicated internal strategy teams).

## Industry Segments
Software/SaaS, Financial Services, Healthcare Technology, and Consulting. These industries face the highest velocity of AI-driven disruption and require the tightest signal-to-noise ratio.

## Job Titles
- VP / Director of Product
- Head of Strategy / Corporate Strategy Lead
- Chief Innovation Officer (CINO)
- Principal / Staff Engineer
- Management Consultant

## Problem Intensity
**Severe constraints on cognitive bandwidth.**
This ICP is expected to have "an AI strategy" by their board or CEO. They spend 5+ hours a week trying to curate disjointed newsletters, Twitter/X feeds, and research papers, but still feel anxious they are missing critical signals or misunderstanding the *implications* for their roadmap. Operational pain is highest when they get blindsided by a competitor's AI feature release.

## Triggers
- Being asked "What is our AI strategy regarding X?" by executive leadership and lacking a coherent, data-backed answer.
- A competitor launching a highly visible, AI-native feature that threatens market share.
- Following a failed internal initiative resulting from acting on superficial AI hype rather than deep signal.
- Annual or quarterly strategic planning cycles.

## Willingness to Pay Signals
- They already pay for premium newsletters (e.g., Stratechery, The Information), specific Substack subscriptions, or specialized industry research reports.
- They have a discretionary professional development or departmental software budget (often >$100/mo) that doesn't require CFO approval.

## Adoption Barriers
- **Trust and Credibility:** If the generated briefs feel like generic LLM summaries rather than sharp, synthesized insights, trust is immediately lost.
- **Workflow Friction:** If they have to log in to a new dashboard daily instead of receiving signals in their existing flow (email/Slack).
- **The "Good Enough" Trap:** Just relying on ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro to summarize URLs manually.

## Why This ICP Is the Best Initial Target
The "Overwhelmed Strategist" in mid-size companies has the highest combination of *urgent operational pain* and *budget autonomy*. Unlike a generic "professional" who might only read news passively out of curiosity, this ICP's job security and bonus structure are tied to successfully navigating the AI transition. They are desperate for relevance over volume.

## Alternative ICPs Worth Exploring
- **Agency/Consultancy Partners:** Smaller consulting firms who need to generate intelligence briefs for *their* clients. They could use AI Signals for Leaders as a white-labeled operational backbone.
- **Venture Capital Analysts:** Junior associates tasked with mapping out AI landscapes and competitor matrices for investment thesis generation.
